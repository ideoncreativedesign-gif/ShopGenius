# Discovery prompts

## Research synthesis

> Here are [N] observations from [source]. Cluster them into behavioral patterns. Separate
> direct evidence from your interpretation, mark which cluster each observation supports, and
> flag contradictions rather than resolving them. Do not add observations that aren't in what I
> gave you.

## Competitive analysis

> Compare these alternatives based on the underlying user job, not feature count. For each:
> target user, core job, primary interaction, trust mechanism, whether AI is load-bearing or
> decorative, business model, and what the user still has to do themselves.
>
> Then: where is there an unmet gap, versus a feature we merely wish competitors had? If a gap
> has stayed open in a market this size, argue for why someone tried it and failed before you
> argue that it's an opportunity.

## Prior art on our interaction model

> Find products that shipped [shoppable in-image components / branching commerce video /
> agent-initiated shopping]. For each, tell me whether it's still shipping, and if not, what's
> publicly known about why it stopped. Distinguish what's documented from what you're
> inferring. If you don't know, say you don't know rather than filling the gap.

## Scenario generation

> Given this shopper and this constraint set, generate 8 concrete shopping scenarios that vary
> along [axis]. Keep them specific enough to be wrong — no composite personas, no "busy
> professional." Then tell me which two are most dangerous to design for and why.

## Thesis attack

> Attack this product thesis. What would have to be true for it to work, and what evidence
> would prove it wrong? Then: what existing behavior already solves this problem well enough
> that our version won't get adopted?
