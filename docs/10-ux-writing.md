# UX Writing

Use Claude to explore language. Don't let it flatten the product's personality into the
median of the internet.

## Voice

Three adjectives, plus one thing we never sound like.

## Banned phrases

Default-prohibited unless the brand genuinely earns them:

"Let AI do the work" · "Magic" · "Powered by AI" · "Unlock your potential" ·
"Your perfect match" · "Curated just for you" · "We've got you covered"

> Product language should describe what the system actually does.

Prefer: *"Narrowed 240 jackets to 6 using your budget and the wedding date"* over
*"We found your perfect jacket."* The first is checkable. The second is a promise we can't keep.

## Copy that carries trust

These strings do more work than any other in the product. Draft each with three alternatives,
then choose deliberately.

| Surface | Job | Draft |
|---|---|---|
| Rationale line | Explain why this, in one line | |
| Uncertainty state | Admit what we don't know without sounding broken | |
| Sponsorship label | Disclose plainly | |
| Exit affordance | Make leaving the narrative feel normal, not like quitting | |
| Undo / rewind | Make reversal obvious | |
| Empty / no-match | Fail usefully | |

## Microcopy system

Rules for: capitalization, product naming, price display, tense, how we refer to the AI (do we
name it? does it say "I"?), and how we address the shopper.

Decide the "does the AI say I" question early and log it — it leaks into every string.
